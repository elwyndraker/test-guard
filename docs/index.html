PrivacyGuard Privacy Policy

Effective date: <date you publish>

PrivacyGuard is a free Android app that blocks known tracking and advertising domains on your phone. This policy explains what happens to your data.

Summary

PrivacyGuard does not collect, store, sell or share any personal data. There is no account and no server run by us.

How the app handles your data

  • Website lookups (DNS). When protection is on, PrivacyGuard uses Android's VpnService to create a local VPN on your phone. Only DNS lookups (the names of websites and services your apps contact) pass through it. Each name is checked against a blocklist on your phone. Blocked names are answered on the phone. Other lookups are forwarded unchanged to your network's normal DNS server, as your phone would do without the app. If that server cannot be found or does not respond, the lookup is sent to Cloudflare's public DNS (1.1.1.1), which handles it under Cloudflare's own privacy policy.
  • Page content. Web pages, videos and other traffic do not pass through PrivacyGuard.
  • Blocked-requests list. The list of blocked names is kept in the phone's memory only, never saved to a server, and is cleared when the app is closed.
  • Blocklist updates. The app downloads the public StevenBlack hosts list from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com). Like any download, GitHub receives your IP address for that request.
  • Settings. Whether protection is on and when the list was last updated are stored on your phone only.

What we do not do

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no crash-reporting SDKs, no remote VPN server. PrivacyGuard does not change your IP address or encrypt your traffic.

Permissions

  • VPN (VpnService): to receive DNS lookups on the device.
  • Internet and network state: to forward allowed lookups and download the blocklist.
  • Notifications and foreground service: to show that protection is running.

Children

PrivacyGuard is not directed at children under 13.
